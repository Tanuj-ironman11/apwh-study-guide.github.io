Unit 8 · c. 1945–1980

COLD WAR ORIGINS

YALTA · TRUMAN DOCTRINE · MARSHALL PLAN · NATO · BERLIN · KOREA

Primary Source · March 12, 1947
"I believe that it must be the policy of the United States to support free peoples who are resisting attempted subjugation by armed minorities or by outside pressures."
— President Harry Truman, Address to Congress — The Truman Doctrine
From Alliance to Division
1945 · WARTIME TO COLD WAR
ORIGINS OF THE COLD WAR
Yalta (Feb 1945)
FDR, Churchill, Stalin divide postwar Europe — seeds of conflict planted
Ideology
US democracy/capitalism vs. Soviet communism — fundamentally incompatible visions
AP Weight
Very High — Cold War origins appear on nearly every AP exam

The Cold War grew from WWII's ashes. The "Big Three" (FDR, Churchill, Stalin) at Yalta (Feb 1945) agreed on postwar Europe but left critical ambiguities. Stalin's imposition of communist governments across Eastern Europe — Poland, Czechoslovakia, East Germany, Hungary, Romania — violated the Yalta agreements on free elections.

Why "Cold"? Both the US and USSR had nuclear weapons after 1949 — direct war meant mutual annihilation. Instead: proxy wars, espionage, propaganda, economic competition, and ideological struggle. The nuclear stalemate defined the entire era.

George Kennan's "Long Telegram" (1946) and "X Article" (1947) formulated containment — the US should not try to roll back communism but contain it wherever it threatened to spread. This became the foundation of US Cold War policy for 45 years.

Yalta ConferenceContainmentIron CurtainGeorge Kennan
⚡ APHIGH FREQ
1947–1952 · USA
TRUMAN DOCTRINE & MARSHALL PLAN
Truman Doctrine
$400M to Greece and Turkey — first explicit US commitment to contain communism anywhere
Marshall Plan
$13 billion to rebuild Western Europe — prosperity as a bulwark against communism
AP Weight
High — the economic dimension of containment

The Truman Doctrine (March 1947) was the first domino of US Cold War policy. When Britain could no longer fund anti-communist forces in Greece and Turkey, Truman asked Congress for $400M and declared the US would support any "free people" resisting communist subjugation. This universalised containment beyond Europe.

Marshall Plan (1948–1952): Secretary of State George Marshall proposed $13 billion in US aid to rebuild Western European economies. The strategic logic: economically desperate populations turn to communism; prosperity is the best inoculation. Stalin rejected the plan for Eastern Europe — deepening the division.

⚡ APHIGH FREQ
1948–1955 · Germany/Europe
BERLIN BLOCKADE, NATO & THE DIVISION OF EUROPE
Berlin Blockade
Stalin blocks land access (1948–49); Western Airlift delivers 200,000+ flights — Stalin backs down
NATO (1949)
First peacetime US military alliance; "an attack on one is an attack on all" — Article 5
AP Weight
High — Berlin as Cold War flashpoint; NATO as US commitment to Europe

Berlin, deep in the Soviet zone, was a Western enclave Stalin couldn't tolerate. In June 1948, Stalin blockaded all land routes. The West responded with a massive airlift — 277,000 flights over 11 months delivered coal, food, and supplies. Stalin ended the blockade in May 1949, handing the US a major propaganda victory.

NATO (April 1949) formalised the Western alliance with the principle of collective defence (Article 5). The US had never joined a peacetime alliance before — this was a revolutionary break with American foreign policy tradition. Germany was divided: West Germany joined NATO (1955); the USSR formed the Warsaw Pact in response.

⚡ APHIGH FREQ
1950–1953 · Korean Peninsula
THE KOREAN WAR
Trigger
North Korea (Soviet-backed) invades South Korea (US-backed), June 25, 1950
Outcome
Armistice (1953) — Korea remains divided at 38th parallel, roughly where it started
AP Weight
High — first hot war of the Cold War; containment in practice

Korea was divided at the 38th parallel after WWII (US occupied south, USSR occupied north). When North Korea invaded in June 1950, Truman saw it as Soviet expansion and committed US forces under the UN flag. Initial collapse → MacArthur's Inchon landing pushes North Korea back → China intervenes → stalemate at 38th parallel.

Significance: The "Forgotten War" killed 36,000 Americans, 500,000+ Koreans. Containment worked — South Korea survived. But the war demonstrated the limits of containment: rollback (MacArthur's push to China's border) risked nuclear war. Truman fired MacArthur for insubordination — civilian control of the military held.

Unit 8 · c. 1945–1980

DECOLONIZATION

AFRICA · ASIA · NON-ALIGNED MOVEMENT · PATTERNS

Primary Source · Bandung Conference, 1955
"We, the peoples of Asia and Africa... declare our support for the rights of all peoples to choose their own political and economic systems."
— Final Communiqué, Bandung Conference, Indonesia, April 1955
The End of European Empire
1947–1975 · GLOBAL
CAUSES OF DECOLONIZATION

WWII broke European empires financially and morally. Nationalist movements that had been building since the 1920s now had a weakened adversary. The US and USSR — both ideologically anti-colonial — pressured European powers. The UN's founding principle of self-determination gave independence movements international legitimacy.

European Weakness: Britain and France emerged from WWII economically devastated and militarily overstretched. Maintaining empires was financially impossible. India and Pakistan (1947) showed that resistance could succeed.
AP Angle: Decolonization was fastest in Asia (1945–55) and Africa (1955–75). Methods ranged from Gandhi-style non-violence (India) to armed revolution (Algeria, Vietnam, Angola). The Cold War complicated independence — both superpowers competed for new nations' allegiance.
⚡ APHIGH FREQ
1947–1954 · SOUTH & SOUTHEAST ASIA
ASIAN DECOLONIZATION

Asia decolonised first. India and Pakistan gained independence from Britain in August 1947 — but partition along religious lines (Hindu India / Muslim Pakistan) caused one of history's largest population transfers and killed hundreds of thousands. Indonesia declared independence from the Netherlands (1945, recognised 1949). Vietnam fought France to a draw at Dien Bien Phu (1954).

Indian Independence & Partition (1947): Gandhi's non-cooperation movement, WWII's costs, and Nehru's leadership forced British withdrawal. Partition killed 200,000–2 million and displaced 10–20 million — demonstrating that independence came at enormous cost.
Dien Bien Phu (1954): Vietnamese communist forces (Viet Minh under Giap) destroyed French garrison — ending French rule in Indochina. Geneva Accords temporarily divided Vietnam at 17th parallel. US refused to implement elections — beginning of US involvement.
⚡ APHIGH FREQ
1956–1975 · AFRICA
THE YEAR OF AFRICA & AFRICAN INDEPENDENCE

1960 — "The Year of Africa" — 17 nations gained independence in a single year. Ghana (1957) under Kwame Nkrumah was sub-Saharan Africa's first independent nation. Algeria won independence from France after a brutal 8-year war (1954–62). By 1975, nearly all of Africa was independent.

Ghana (1957) & Nkrumah: First sub-Saharan African independence. Nkrumah promoted Pan-Africanism — African unity as a geopolitical force. "Seek ye first the political kingdom" — political independence must precede economic development.
Congo Crisis (1960): Belgian Congo gained independence June 1960. Within days, army mutinied; Katanga province seceded; Patrice Lumumba (first prime minister) was assassinated — with CIA involvement. Shows how Cold War superpowers exploited decolonization for strategic advantage.
⚡ APHIGH FREQ
1955 · GLOBAL SOUTH
NON-ALIGNED MOVEMENT

Bandung Conference (1955): 29 Asian and African nations — representing more than half the world's population — met in Indonesia. Led by Nehru (India), Nkrumah (Ghana), Tito (Yugoslavia), Nasser (Egypt), and Sukarno (Indonesia). They rejected both US and Soviet blocs — forming a "Third World" that refused superpower alignment.

Key Principles: Anti-colonialism, national sovereignty, non-interference, peaceful coexistence. The NAM gave newly independent nations a platform to demand economic justice, oppose the nuclear arms race, and reject Cold War binary thinking.
AP Angle: The "Third World" was not passive — it was an active political force. Both superpowers competed desperately for NAM nations' allegiance through aid, military support, and ideological propaganda. This competition often led to proxy conflicts in newly independent states.
⚡ APHIGH FREQ
Unit 8 · c. 1945–1980

KEY FIGURES

SUPERPOWER LEADERS · INDEPENDENCE LEADERS · REVOLUTIONARIES

Superpower Leaders
1884–1972 · USA
HARRY TRUMAN
US President 1945–1953
Inherited the presidency on FDR's death (April 1945), then made the most consequential decisions of the Cold War's opening phase. Authorised atomic bombs on Japan. Truman Doctrine (1947) committed US to contain communism globally. Marshall Plan rebuilt Western Europe. NATO founded. Led the US in Korea.
⚡ APHIGH FREQ
1878–1953 · USSR
JOSEPH STALIN
Soviet Leader (Post-WWII)
Established Soviet satellite states across Eastern Europe (1945–48). Rejected the Marshall Plan for Soviet bloc. Ordered the Berlin Blockade (1948). Detonated the Soviet atomic bomb (1949) — ending US nuclear monopoly. Backed North Korea's invasion (1950). Death (1953) opened a brief thaw — Khrushchev's "de-Stalinisation" followed.
⚡ APHIGH FREQ
1894–1971 · USSR
NIKITA KHRUSHCHEV
Soviet Premier 1953–1964
Denounced Stalin (1956 Secret Speech — "de-Stalinisation"). Ordered construction of the Berlin Wall (1961). Initiated and then backed down from the Cuban Missile Crisis (1962) — the Cold War's closest moment to nuclear war. "We will bury you." Ousted in 1964 for the Cuba retreat. His era defined the most dangerous phase of the Cold War.
⚡ APHIGH FREQ
1913–1994 · USA
RICHARD NIXON
US President 1969–1974
Architect of Détente — relaxation of Cold War tensions. Nixon's China visit (1972) was a geopolitical masterstroke: splitting the USSR-China alliance. SALT I arms limitation treaty (1972). Withdrew US from Vietnam (1973). Watergate forced resignation (1974). Despite domestic scandal, Nixon reshaped Cold War strategy more than any president since Truman.
⚡ APMED FREQ
Independence Leaders & Revolutionaries
1890–1969 · Vietnam
HO CHI MINH
Vietnamese Communist Leader
Led Vietnamese independence movement against France, then the US. Founded the Viet Minh (1941) and led it to victory at Dien Bien Phu (1954). As leader of North Vietnam, oversaw the Vietnam War against the US-backed South. Died in 1969 before reunification (1975). Blended nationalism and communism into a uniquely Vietnamese resistance that outlasted two superpowers.
⚡ APHIGH FREQ
1909–1972 · Ghana
KWAME NKRUMAH
Ghanaian Independence Leader
Led Ghana to independence (1957) — the first sub-Saharan African nation. Pioneer of Pan-Africanism: the idea that all African peoples share a common identity and should unite politically. "Seek ye first the political kingdom." Hosted the 1958 All-African People's Conference. Overthrown in 1966 military coup. His vision inspired independence movements across the continent.
⚡ APHIGH FREQ
1889–1964 · India
JAWAHARLAL NEHRU
First Prime Minister of India
India's first prime minister (1947–1964). Co-founded the Non-Aligned Movement at Bandung (1955) — refusing to align with either superpower. Promoted "Panchsheel" (Five Principles of Peaceful Coexistence). Led India through partition's traumatic aftermath and the early Cold War while maintaining genuine non-alignment. His foreign policy became a model for newly independent nations.
⚡ APHIGH FREQ
1926–2016 · Cuba
FIDEL CASTRO
Cuban Revolutionary Leader
Led the Cuban Revolution (1959) ousting US-backed dictator Batista. Aligned Cuba with the Soviet Union after the US attempted to overthrow him (Bay of Pigs, 1961). Agreed to host Soviet nuclear missiles — triggering the Cuban Missile Crisis (1962). Cuba became the most significant Soviet ally in the Western Hemisphere, inspiring revolutionary movements across Latin America.
⚡ APHIGH FREQ
1893–1976 · China
MAO ZEDONG
Chairman of the PRC
Established the People's Republic of China (1949) — the world's most populous communist state. Great Leap Forward (1958–62) attempted rapid industrialisation through collectivisation — famine killed 15–55 million. Cultural Revolution (1966–76) destroyed Chinese culture and killed 500,000–2 million. Sino-Soviet Split (1960s) showed communism was not monolithic — and opened the door for Nixon's China visit (1972).
⚡ APHIGH FREQ
Unit 8 · c. 1945–1980

PROXY WARS

KOREA · VIETNAM · CUBA · COLD WAR BY OTHER MEANS

THE COLD WAR'S DEFINING PARADOX
NO DIRECT WAR, MILLIONS DEAD
Nuclear deterrence (MAD) prevented direct US-Soviet conflict. Instead, the superpowers fought through proxies — funding, arming, and advising local forces in Korea, Vietnam, Angola, Afghanistan, and dozens of other conflicts. The Cold War was "cold" only for Americans and Soviets; for Koreans, Vietnamese, and Angolans, it was devastatingly hot. An estimated 20–30 million people died in Cold War proxy conflicts.
Key Proxy Conflicts
1950–1953
THE KOREAN WAR
North Korea (Soviet/Chinese-backed) vs. South Korea (US/UN-backed). 36,000 US dead; 500,000+ Korean dead. MacArthur pushed too far north → China intervened. Armistice at 38th parallel — no peace treaty. Korea remains the unfinished Cold War conflict. Key AP point: demonstrates both the necessity of containment AND the danger of escalation.
⚡ APHIGH FREQ
1955–1975
THE VIETNAM WAR
US commitment escalated from advisors (Eisenhower) to 500,000 troops (Johnson, 1968). Gulf of Tonkin Resolution (1964) gave LBJ unlimited war powers. Tet Offensive (1968) — militarily defeated but psychologically shattered US public support. Nixon's "Vietnamisation" + Paris Peace Accords (1973) = US withdrawal. North Vietnam reunified by force (1975). First US military defeat — 58,000 Americans, ~3M Vietnamese dead.
⚡ APHIGH FREQ
OCTOBER 1962
CUBAN MISSILE CRISIS
Soviet nuclear missiles discovered in Cuba by U-2 spy plane (October 14, 1962). Kennedy imposed naval blockade. 13 days in which the world held its breath — the closest point in history to nuclear war. Khrushchev withdrew missiles; Kennedy secretly agreed to remove US missiles from Turkey. Both sides stepped back from the abyss. Immediate aftermath: Hotline established; Partial Test Ban Treaty (1963).
⚡ APHIGH FREQ
1961–1975 · AFRICA/LATIN AMERICA
THIRD WORLD PROXY CONFLICTS
Angola Civil War (1975): US backed UNITA/FNLA; USSR and Cuba backed MPLA — Cubans deployed 50,000 troops. Congo/Zaire: CIA assassinated Lumumba; US backed Mobutu's dictatorship. Iran: CIA overthrew Mossadegh (1953) and reinstated the Shah — whose oppressive rule ultimately produced the 1979 Islamic Revolution. US interventions repeatedly created long-term blowback that reshaped the world.
⚡ APMED FREQ
LESSONS & PATTERNS
WHY THE US OFTEN FAILED IN PROXY WARS
Vietnam, Korea, Angola — Cold War proxy conflicts were often lost or inconclusive. Key reasons: (1) Nationalist movements were stronger than ideological allegiance; (2) Local forces fighting for their homes were more motivated than conscripts fighting in distant jungles; (3) Containment logic blinded policymakers to local context; (4) Public opinion in democracies had limits — 58,000 dead produced the "Vietnam Syndrome." For AP: compare decolonisation struggles with Cold War proxy logic.
⚡ APHIGH FREQ

NUCLEAR AGE & DÉTENTE

MAD · ARMS RACE · SPACE RACE · DÉTENTE · OIL SHOCKS

Primary Source · JFK, 1963
"Above all, while defending our vital interests, nuclear powers must avert those confrontations which bring an adversary to a choice of either a humiliating retreat or a nuclear war."
— President Kennedy, American University Commencement Address, June 10, 1963
Living Under the Bomb
1945–1980 · GLOBAL
MUTUALLY ASSURED DESTRUCTION

MAD was the Cold War's defining strategic doctrine: if either superpower launched a nuclear first strike, the other retained enough weapons to destroy the attacker many times over. This made nuclear war "unwinnable" — and therefore paradoxically stabilising. Neither side would start a war they couldn't win.

The US atomic monopoly lasted only 4 years (1945–1949). The Soviet bomb (1949), hydrogen bombs (1952 US, 1953 USSR), ICBMs (1957), submarine-launched missiles (1960s), and MIRVs (multiple warheads, 1970s) continuously escalated the stakes. By 1980, the US and USSR had ~50,000 nuclear weapons between them — enough to destroy humanity many times over. "Overkill" became a technical term.

⚡ APHIGH FREQ
1957–1969 · US vs. USSR
THE SPACE RACE

The space race was simultaneously a scientific achievement and a Cold War propaganda war. Sputnik (October 4, 1957) — the first artificial satellite — shocked Americans. If Soviets could put a satellite into orbit, they could drop a nuclear warhead anywhere on Earth. Sputnik triggered the creation of NASA (1958) and massively expanded US science education funding.

Yuri Gagarin became first human in space (April 1961). Kennedy responded with the Moon pledge (May 1961). Apollo 11 landed on the Moon (July 20, 1969) — "One small step for man, one giant leap for mankind." The Moon landing gave the US an enormous propaganda victory. For AP: the space race as Cold War competition AND scientific milestone.

⚡ APHIGH FREQ
1969–1979 · USA & USSR
DÉTENTE

After the Cuban Missile Crisis (1962), both sides recognised the danger of constant confrontation. Nixon and Kissinger's policy of Détente ("relaxation") sought to manage, rather than win, the Cold War. Nixon's China visit (1972) was the masterstroke — playing the Sino-Soviet split to create a triangular balance of power. SALT I (1972) limited nuclear weapons for the first time.

Helsinki Accords (1975) recognised European borders AND included human rights provisions — which Soviet dissidents used to organise resistance. Détente ended with the Soviet invasion of Afghanistan (1979) and the Iranian Revolution (1979) — Carter's foreign policy failures that opened the door for Reagan's Cold War reinvigoration.

⚡ APHIGH FREQ
1973 · GLOBAL
OIL CRISIS & ECONOMIC SHOCKS

OPEC (Organisation of the Petroleum Exporting Countries) imposed an oil embargo (October 1973) after the US supported Israel in the Yom Kippur War. Oil prices quadrupled overnight. Gas shortages, stagflation (inflation + unemployment), and the end of postwar prosperity rocked Western economies. The crisis demonstrated the economic power newly independent nations could wield through commodity control.

The oil crisis also revealed the limits of US power and accelerated the search for energy alternatives. For AP: the OPEC crisis as a key example of how decolonised, resource-rich nations could project power — and as a cause of both the Iran Revolution (oil wealth + resentment of US support for the Shah) and broader challenges to Western hegemony in the 1970s.

CHRONOLOGICAL TIMELINE

KEY EVENTS 1945–1980 · FILTER BY CATEGORY

Feb 1945
YALTA CONFERENCE ⚡ AP
FDR, Churchill, Stalin meet to plan postwar Europe. Germany to be divided. Free elections promised for Eastern Europe — Stalin will not honour this. Seeds of Cold War planted in the Allied victory conference.
Aug 6 & 9, 1945
ATOMIC BOMBS — HIROSHIMA & NAGASAKI ⚡ AP
~200,000 killed; Japan surrenders. The nuclear age begins — every subsequent Cold War calculation is shaped by atomic weapons. MAD becomes the core strategic doctrine.
Aug 1947
INDIAN INDEPENDENCE & PARTITION ⚡ AP
British India partitioned into India and Pakistan along religious lines. 10–20 million displaced; 200,000–2M killed in communal violence. First major decolonisation event — demonstrated both the possibility and the cost of independence.
March 1947
TRUMAN DOCTRINE ⚡ AP
Truman asks Congress for $400M for Greece and Turkey — declaring the US will support free peoples resisting communist subjugation. Containment becomes official US policy.
1947–1952
MARSHALL PLAN ⚡ AP
$13 billion US aid rebuilds Western European economies. Strategic logic: prosperity prevents communism. Stalin refuses to allow Eastern European participation — deepens Iron Curtain division.
1948–1949
BERLIN BLOCKADE & AIRLIFT ⚡ AP
Stalin blocks land access to West Berlin. Western Airlift delivers 277,000 flights over 11 months. Stalin backs down — US wins Cold War's first major test of resolve. NATO founded April 1949.
Aug 1949
SOVIET ATOMIC BOMB ⚡ AP
USSR tests its first nuclear weapon — 4 years after Hiroshima. US nuclear monopoly ends. MAD becomes a permanent condition. Aided by espionage (Rosenbergs). Nuclear arms race officially begins.
Oct 1949
PEOPLE'S REPUBLIC OF CHINA FOUNDED ⚡ AP
Mao's communists defeat Nationalists in civil war. Chiang Kai-shek flees to Taiwan. 600M people turn communist — US asks "Who lost China?" Mao signs Sino-Soviet Treaty of Friendship (1950). Asia transformed.
1950–1953
KOREAN WAR ⚡ AP
North Korea invades South. US/UN forces push back; China intervenes. Armistice at 38th parallel. 36,000 Americans dead; 500,000+ Koreans. Containment in practice — costly but successful.
Apr 1955
BANDUNG CONFERENCE — NON-ALIGNED MOVEMENT ⚡ AP
29 Asian and African nations refuse to align with either superpower. Nehru, Nkrumah, Sukarno, Tito, Nasser create the "Third World." Decolonised nations assert collective political identity.
Mar 1957
GHANA INDEPENDENCE — NKRUMAH ⚡ AP
First sub-Saharan African independence. Kwame Nkrumah leads Ghana to independence from Britain. "Year of Africa" 1960 follows — 17 more nations independent. Pan-Africanism inspires the continent.
Oct 4, 1957
SPUTNIK LAUNCHES SPACE RACE ⚡ AP
USSR launches first artificial satellite. Shock in the US: if Soviets can orbit a satellite, they can deliver nuclear warheads anywhere. NASA created 1958. Space Race begins — geopolitics by rocket.
Jan 1959
CUBAN REVOLUTION — CASTRO
Fidel Castro's guerrillas defeat US-backed Batista. Cuba declares socialist state. Bay of Pigs invasion (1961) fails catastrophically. Cuba becomes Soviet ally 90 miles from Florida — the Cold War in America's backyard.
Aug 1961
BERLIN WALL BUILT ⚡ AP
East Germany, directed by Khrushchev, builds a wall to stop East Germans fleeing to the West. 3.5 million had already fled — the "brain drain" was crippling East Germany. The Wall becomes the defining symbol of the Cold War division.
Oct 1962
CUBAN MISSILE CRISIS ⚡ AP
13 days of the closest the world came to nuclear war. Soviet missiles in Cuba; Kennedy naval blockade; back-channel negotiations; Khrushchev withdraws. Hotline established. Partial Test Ban Treaty 1963 follows.
July 20, 1969
APOLLO 11 — MOON LANDING ⚡ AP
Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin land on the Moon — "one small step for man." US wins the Space Race decisively. Greatest propaganda victory of the Cold War. 600M people watch on television.
1969–1979
DÉTENTE — NIXON, KISSINGER, BREZHNEV ⚡ AP
Nixon visits China (1972) — splits Sino-Soviet alliance. SALT I (1972) limits nuclear weapons. Helsinki Accords (1975) recognise human rights. Détente ends with Soviet invasion of Afghanistan (1979).
1964–1975
VIETNAM WAR — US DEFEAT ⚡ AP
Gulf of Tonkin (1964); 500,000 US troops at peak (1968); Tet Offensive shatters public support; Nixon withdraws (1973); Saigon falls (1975). 58,000 US dead; ~3M Vietnamese dead. First US military defeat.
Oct 1973
OPEC OIL EMBARGO ⚡ AP
Arab OPEC nations embargo oil to US after Yom Kippur War support of Israel. Oil prices quadruple. Stagflation rocks Western economies. Demonstrates power of newly independent nations controlling global commodities.
1979
IRANIAN REVOLUTION ⚡ AP
Shah Pahlavi (US-backed) overthrown; Ayatollah Khomeini establishes Islamic Republic. Iran hostage crisis (444 days). Demonstrates the limits of US power and how Cold War interventions (CIA coup 1953) generate long-term blowback.
